# How to Think With the Dashboard

The dashboard should answer questions, not merely display counts.

## 1. Define the analytical universe first

Ask:

- Am I studying all Alzheimer's clinical research?
- Or only the current therapeutic pipeline?
- Or only industry-sponsored active therapeutics?

Changing the universe can change the conclusion.

## 2. Mechanism crowding is not the same as commercial attractiveness

A crowded mechanism may mean:

- strong biological validation,
- strong commercial interest,
- or late entry into a mature race.

An uncrowded mechanism may mean:

- differentiated opportunity,
- weak validation,
- or technical difficulty.

Never interpret counts alone.

## 3. Phase is not enough

Two Phase 2 studies may have very different information value.

Compare:

- sample size,
- biomarker enrichment,
- control group,
- treatment duration,
- primary endpoint,
- safety monitoring,
- geography,
- sponsor capabilities.

## 4. Primary completion is a research lead, not a guaranteed catalyst date

Use it to create a candidate event calendar.

Then verify the actual expected readout in:

- company investor materials,
- earnings calls,
- conference schedules,
- press releases.

## 5. Sponsor class changes the question

For an academic study, ask whether it validates a mechanism or method.

For an industry study, additionally ask:

- which asset is being built?
- what is the competitive positioning?
- what capital is required to reach the next value-inflection point?

## 6. Your first recurring exercise

Every week choose 3 studies:

1. one late-stage asset,
2. one emerging mechanism,
3. one unusual trial design.

For each, write five lines:

- What changed?
- Why does the study exist?
- What evidence would make the asset more valuable?
- What evidence would break the thesis?
- What should I monitor next?
